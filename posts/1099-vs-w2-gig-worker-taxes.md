## Uber, Lyft, DoorDash, Instacart, and Grubhub generally treat drivers as independent contractors

That single choice - 1099 instead of W-2 - changes how you are paid, how you are taxed, and what you have to do in January through April.

If you only remember one thing: **a 1099 is not a paycheck stub**. It is a report of money you already received, with no taxes removed.

## What the forms actually mean

### W-2

A W-2 comes from an employer. They withheld federal income tax, Social Security, and Medicare (and often state tax). They paid the other half of Social Security and Medicare. You report wages on Form 1040. You usually do not file a Schedule C for that job.

### 1099-NEC and 1099-K

Gig platforms may send:

- **1099-NEC** for nonemployee compensation
- **1099-K** for payments processed through a third-party network

You must report the income whether a form arrives or not. If the form is wrong, you still start from your own records and then reconcile.

A 1099 does not say how much tax you owe. It just tells the IRS a number they can match against your return.

## Self-employment tax, in plain English

Self-employment tax is Social Security and Medicare for people who do not have an employer. The combined rate is 15.3% on 92.35% of net profit, until you hit the annual Social Security wage base (for 2025 that is $176,100).

You do get to deduct the employer-equivalent half of self-employment tax on Form 1040. That helps a little. It does not make the 15.3% disappear.

Net profit is the key word. Miles, bags, and phone costs lower the profit that this tax applies to. That is why a mileage log is not extra credit. It is how you avoid paying Social Security tax on gasoline.

## Schedule C is your gig-work profit and loss

Most drivers report Uber/Lyft/DoorDash activity on **Schedule C**. Income at the top, expenses below, net profit at the bottom. That net profit flows to Schedule SE (self-employment tax) and to Form 1040.

If you drive for three apps, you usually still have **one** rideshare/delivery business, not three separate companies. Combine the income. Combine the shared expenses.

## Benefits you lose when you leave W-2 land

Taxes are only half the story. Contractors typically do not get:

- Employer health insurance
- Employer 401(k) match
- Paid sick time
- Unemployment insurance (in most cases)
- Workers compensation (you are the business)
- Someone else tracking your hours or mileage

You can rebuild pieces of this: a marketplace health plan, a SEP-IRA or solo 401(k), an emergency fund that is your sick day.

## Hybrid workers: W-2 by day, 1099 by night

A lot of drivers already have a day job. The W-2 withholding does **not** automatically cover the gig profit.

Common mistake: "I already have taxes taken out at my warehouse job, so the DoorDash money is free." The warehouse withholding was calculated on warehouse wages. The IRS still expects tax on the Dasher profit.

Fixes that work:

- Raise W-2 withholding with a new Form W-4 so your day job over-withholds enough to cover the side hustle
- Or make quarterly estimates just on the gig profit
- Or do a mix

## Mistakes that trigger letters

- Reporting only the 1099 total and ignoring cash-app tips or missing weeks
- Ignoring a 1099-K that the IRS also received
- Deducting 100% of a personal car with no mileage log
- Forgetting state returns
- Filing as if the gig were a hobby with no Schedule C

## A filing-season workflow for 1099 drivers

1. Download annual statements from every app in January
2. Export mileage and receipts
3. List all 1099s in a simple table: payer, form type, box amount
4. Reconcile to your bank deposits
5. Complete Schedule C
6. Let software or a preparer generate Schedule SE and estimates for next year
7. Pay what you owe or set up a plan
8. Immediately start saving for the current year

## Bottom line

W-2 means someone else ran payroll. 1099 means **you** are payroll. You get deductions W-2 employees do not, and you get a tax bill W-2 employees never see in one lump. Treat the app like a customer, treat yourself like a tiny business, and the 1099 vs W-2 confusion gets a lot smaller.
