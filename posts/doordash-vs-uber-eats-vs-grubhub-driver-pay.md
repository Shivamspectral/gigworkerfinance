## Why platform choice matters for your bottom line

Most active delivery drivers work multiple platforms simultaneously, but knowing which one performs best in your specific market and time slot helps you prioritize where to focus. Pay structures, tip culture, order volume, and bonus frequency differ meaningfully across DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Grubhub.

## DoorDash pay structure

DoorDash uses a base pay system that varies based on time, distance, and desirability of the order. Base pay typically ranges from $2 to $10 per order before tips.

**How DoorDash pay works:**
- Base pay: set by DoorDash based on estimated time and distance
- Tips: 100% passed through to the driver
- Peak Pay: additional per-order bonus during high-demand periods, shown on the earnings tab
- Challenges and Streaks: complete a certain number of deliveries in a period for bonus pay

**What drivers actually report earning:**
Most active DoorDash drivers in mid-size US markets report earning between $15 and $22 per hour before expenses during reasonable working hours, with peak periods pushing higher.

**DoorDash strengths:**
- Largest market share of any food delivery platform in the US, meaning more orders in most markets
- Generally reliable order volume during lunch and dinner peaks
- Transparent earning breakdown per order before acceptance
- Active bonus and challenge structure

**DoorDash weaknesses:**
- Base pay can be low on some orders, requiring tips to make them worthwhile
- Acceptance rate pressure through Top Dasher program affects access to scheduling in some markets
- Customer support quality is inconsistent

## Uber Eats pay structure

Uber Eats calculates pay based on a pickup fee, dropoff fee, and distance traveled, with 100% of tips passed to drivers.

**How Uber Eats pay works:**
- Pickup fee: flat amount for picking up the order
- Dropoff fee: flat amount for completing delivery
- Distance pay: per-mile rate for the delivery distance
- Tips: fully passed through
- Promotions: surge pricing during high demand, Boost multipliers, and quests (complete X deliveries for bonus)

**What drivers actually report earning:**
Uber Eats hourly earnings are broadly similar to DoorDash in most markets, typically $14 to $21 per hour before expenses, with significant market variation.

**Uber Eats strengths:**
- Integrated with the broader Uber ecosystem, so drivers can switch between rideshare and delivery within the same app
- Strong presence in urban markets and college towns
- Boost and surge multipliers can significantly increase pay during peak periods
- Generally good order volume in markets where Uber has strong brand presence

**Uber Eats weaknesses:**
- Pay per order can be less transparent than DoorDash before acceptance
- In some markets, Uber Eats order volume lags behind DoorDash
- Support experiences are mixed among drivers

## Grubhub pay structure

Grubhub uses a pay formula based on time and mileage, with tips added on top.

**How Grubhub pay works:**
- Time pay: per-minute rate while on an active delivery
- Mileage pay: per-mile rate for the delivery distance
- Tips: 100% passed through
- Grubhub Guarantee: minimum hourly pay guarantee in some markets during scheduled blocks

**What drivers actually report earning:**
Grubhub earnings vary more by market than either DoorDash or Uber Eats. In markets where Grubhub has strong restaurant partnerships, drivers report solid earnings. In markets where Grubhub has lower penetration, order volume can be frustratingly low.

**Grubhub strengths:**
- Time-based pay component means longer waits at restaurants do not penalize drivers as severely
- Minimum pay guarantee in some markets during scheduled delivery blocks
- Strong in specific markets, particularly in the Northeast and Chicago

**Grubhub weaknesses:**
- Market presence is much weaker than DoorDash nationally
- Order volume highly variable by market
- Scheduling system (blocks) can be restrictive compared to on-demand availability on other platforms

## Which platform pays more: the honest answer

No single platform pays universally more in every market. The truthful answer depends on:

**Your specific city:**
In New York, Chicago, and Boston, Grubhub historically has strong order volume and competitive pay. In most of the rest of the country, DoorDash dominates by order volume. Uber Eats performs best in markets with strong overall Uber brand presence.

**Time of day:**
DoorDash peak pay and Uber Eats surge multipliers can make either platform significantly higher paying during specific demand windows, while Grubhub's time-based pay helps during slower periods with longer restaurant waits.

**Your acceptance rate strategy:**
DoorDash's Top Dasher benefits depend on maintaining a high acceptance rate. Drivers who decline low-paying orders more freely may find Uber Eats or Grubhub less restrictive.

## Practical recommendation for most drivers

Start with DoorDash as your primary platform given its nationwide order volume advantage. Add Uber Eats as a secondary platform for multi-apping during slow periods. Add Grubhub if your specific market shows strong Grubhub order volume (ask local drivers or check Gridwise data for your area before investing time learning the platform).

## Tax treatment across all three platforms

From a tax perspective, income from all three platforms is combined on one Schedule C as a single delivery driving business. All business miles driven for any platform count toward your mileage deduction. There is no tax advantage to working one platform versus another - the deduction and reporting rules are identical.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I work all three platforms simultaneously?**
Yes, and many drivers do during slow periods, going online on all three and accepting whichever sends the first order. The workflow requires some app management but reduces idle time significantly.

**Does driving for multiple platforms affect my ratings on each?**
Only if managing multiple apps causes late pickups or other service quality issues. Done carefully with proper workflow discipline, ratings should not be affected.

**Which platform has the best driver support?**
Driver support quality varies significantly by market and changes over time. DoorDash and Uber Eats generally have more developed support systems, though individual experiences vary widely.

**Should I prioritize the platform with the best bonus structure?**
Bonus structures change frequently and vary by market. Base pay per order and order volume in your specific area are more reliable indicators of long-term earnings than promotional bonus structures that may change.

## Bottom line

DoorDash leads in nationwide order volume making it the default starting point for most drivers. Uber Eats performs strongly in urban markets and integrates well for drivers who also do rideshare. Grubhub is worth adding in specific strong markets but is not a primary platform for most drivers outside those areas. Run all three simultaneously during slow periods and use whichever sends orders first to minimize idle time and maximize earnings per online hour.
