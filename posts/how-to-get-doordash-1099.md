## Where your DoorDash 1099 actually comes from

DoorDash doesn't mail tax forms itself. It uses Stripe, its payment processor, to issue a **1099-NEC** (Nonemployee Compensation) to Dashers through a portal called Stripe Express. If you've never logged into Stripe Express, that's usually why you can't find your form.

The 1099-NEC reports your gross DoorDash earnings for the calendar year: base pay, promotions, and tips paid through the app. It does **not** subtract your mileage or expenses – that's your job on Schedule C. If you want the full filing walkthrough once you have the form, start with [how to file DoorDash taxes](/blog/how-to-file-doordash-taxes).

## Step-by-step: downloading your 1099 from Stripe Express

1. **Check your email for a Stripe invite.** DoorDash sends an email (usually in December or early January) inviting you to set up or confirm your Stripe Express account. Search your inbox and spam folder for "Stripe" and "DoorDash".
2. **Confirm your tax details.** Log in and verify your legal name, address, and SSN or EIN. If anything is wrong here, the form will be wrong – fix it before the form generates.
3. **Choose e-delivery.** Opt in to electronic delivery so the form appears in the portal the moment it's issued. If you don't, Stripe mails a paper copy, which takes longer and gets lost more often.
4. **Download the PDF.** Once issued, the 1099-NEC shows under the Tax Forms tab. Save a copy somewhere outside your email.

Forms are required to be issued by **January 31**. If it's early February and you still see nothing, move to the next section.

## Why you might not receive a 1099 at all

There are three common reasons:

**You earned under the reporting threshold.** For years the 1099-NEC threshold has been $600 in gross payments for the calendar year. Congress has changed 1099 thresholds more than once recently, so check DoorDash's tax help page for the figure that applies to the year you're filing. If you were under it, no form is generated – but see below, because you still owe tax on that income.

**Your Stripe Express account was never set up or has old details.** Stripe can't deliver a form to an account that doesn't exist or has a dead email. Log in, update your info, and the form will regenerate or become visible.

**The paper copy went to an old address.** If you moved and didn't update your address in Stripe or the Dasher app, the mailed form went to your previous address. Switch to e-delivery and download it instead.

## What to do if you earned under the threshold

This is where a lot of Dashers get it wrong: **no 1099 does not mean no taxes.**

The threshold controls whether DoorDash has to *report* your income to the IRS. It has nothing to do with whether *you* have to report it. All self-employment income is taxable, and once your net profit from all gig work combined hits **$400**, you owe self-employment tax on it. The [self-employment tax explainer](/blog/self-employment-tax-explained-gig-drivers) covers how that number works.

**How to file without a 1099:**

1. Open the Dasher app and go to Earnings. Pull your weekly or monthly totals for the full calendar year.
2. Add them up to get your gross. Add any cash tips (those never appear in the app).
3. Enter that gross on Schedule C, Line 1, exactly as you would if a 1099 existed.
4. Deduct your mileage and expenses as normal – see the [DoorDash tax deductions list](/blog/doordash-tax-deductions-2025).

You do not attach a 1099 to your return anyway, so filing without one is procedurally identical.

## What to do if your 1099 is wrong

Compare the gross on your 1099-NEC against your own Dasher earnings total. Small differences usually come from timing – a payout initiated in late December that landed in January. Large differences need to be resolved before you file. [How to handle a wrong 1099-K](/blog/how-to-handle-wrong-1099-k) walks through requesting a corrected form and what to do if DoorDash won't issue one in time.

Never report a lower figure than your 1099 shows to make your return match your own records. The IRS matches the 1099 to your return automatically, and a mismatch in that direction triggers a notice.

## Deadlines that matter

- **January 31** – DoorDash/Stripe deadline to issue your 1099-NEC
- **April 15** – individual return due (or file an extension by this date)
- **Quarterly** – if you owe more than $1,000 for the year, you should already be paying [quarterly estimated taxes](/blog/quarterly-estimated-taxes-gig-workers) rather than waiting for the 1099

## Frequently asked questions

**Does DoorDash send a 1099-K or a 1099-NEC?**
Dashers receive a 1099-NEC. The 1099-K applies to payment-card and third-party network transactions and is more common for marketplace sellers.

**I dashed in two states. Do I get two forms?**
No – one 1099-NEC covers all your DoorDash earnings for the year. State allocation, if relevant, is handled on your state returns. See [state taxes for gig drivers](/blog/state-taxes-gig-drivers-by-state).

**Can I get a copy of last year's 1099?**
Yes. Prior-year forms stay available in Stripe Express under Tax Forms. If you've lost access to that account entirely, you can request a Wage and Income Transcript from the IRS, which shows every 1099 filed under your SSN.

**Do cash tips go on my 1099?**
No. Only tips paid through the app are included. Cash tips are still taxable income and must be added to your gross on Schedule C.
