## Set this up before your first week of driving

Most new gig drivers focus entirely on the app mechanics of driving and delivering - how to accept requests, navigate to destinations, and maintain ratings. The financial setup gets skipped until the first April tax bill arrives as a shock.

This checklist covers everything you need to set up financially before or during your first week so that nothing catches you off guard later.

## Week one financial checklist

### Account setup

**Open a dedicated business checking account**
Route all gig platform deposits here, separate from your personal spending account. Free options include Found, Novo, and Lili, all designed for gig workers and freelancers with no monthly fees.

**Open a dedicated tax savings account**
This is separate from both your personal account and your business account. Label it explicitly as your tax fund. Ally, Marcus, or Capital One 360 offer high-yield savings accounts with no fees earning 4-5% APY.

**Open an emergency fund account**
A third savings account for genuine unexpected expenses. Start with a goal of $1,000, building toward three to six months of living expenses over time.

### App setup

**Download a mileage tracking app**
Stride is the recommended starting point - completely free, no trip limits, tracks both mileage and expenses. Enable location permissions to Always Allow (not just while using the app).

**Download your primary gig platform apps**
Set up direct deposit to your new business checking account, not your personal account.

**Set up a simple expense logging system**
Either within Stride, a separate expense app, or a basic spreadsheet. You need somewhere to record receipt photos and expense amounts immediately when purchases happen.

### Insurance verification

**Contact your auto insurer**
Disclose that you are driving for a gig platform and ask specifically about a rideshare or TNC (transportation network company) endorsement. Do this before your first driving session. Operating without proper coverage and getting into an accident can result in a denied claim.

**Verify your health insurance situation**
If you are moving from W-2 employment to full-time gig work, you likely have a COBRA option for 60 days after leaving your job. Evaluate COBRA cost versus marketplace plans through healthcare.gov before your employer coverage ends.

## First month financial habits to establish

### The 25-30% rule (non-negotiable)

The moment any gig platform deposits money into your business account, transfer 25-30% immediately to your tax savings account. Not at the end of the week. Not when you remember. Immediately.

Set up an automatic rule if your bank allows it, or make it a manual habit triggered by every deposit notification. This single habit prevents every first-year tax surprise.

### Weekly five-minute review

Every week (pick the same day and time), spend five minutes:
1. Opening your mileage app and classifying any unclassified trips as business or personal
2. Logging any business expenses from the past week
3. Confirming your tax savings balance grew appropriately relative to the week's income
4. Noting anything unusual (a large expense coming, a slower week, a bonus received)

This weekly review catches problems early - a mileage app that stopped tracking, an expense you almost forgot, a week where you underfunded the tax account.

### Track your actual hourly rate

Once per week, calculate your real earnings per online hour for that week. Divide total gross earnings by total hours the app was active (not just time with passengers or on deliveries - total online time).

This number is your most important performance metric. Tracking it weekly shows whether your timing, positioning, and platform choices are improving over time.

## First quarter financial tasks

### Set up quarterly estimated taxes

By your first quarterly deadline (April 15 for most drivers starting in Q1), you should be ready to make your first federal estimated payment. Use IRS Direct Pay at irs.gov.

Estimate your quarterly payment using one of these methods:
- Safe harbor: pay 25% of last year's total tax liability (works if you have a prior year return to reference)
- Current year estimate: estimate your annual gig net profit and calculate 25-30% of that for the year, then divide by four for each quarterly payment

Also check your state's estimated payment requirements and set up state payments separately through your state tax agency's online portal.

### Check your mileage and expense totals

After your first full month of driving, review:
- Total business miles tracked
- Total business expenses logged
- Total income received
- Total amount saved in tax fund

Calculate your estimated annualized deduction (monthly miles x 70 cents x 12) to understand what you are on track to claim at year end. If the number seems low relative to your income, verify your mileage tracking is working correctly.

## Annual financial tasks

### January: gather tax documents

- Download annual earnings summaries from every gig platform
- Collect all 1099-NEC and 1099-K forms (typically arrive by January 31)
- Export your full-year mileage log from your tracking app
- Organize receipts and expense records by category

### February to March: file your return

- Complete Schedule C (gig income and expenses)
- Complete Schedule SE (self-employment tax)
- Complete Form 1040 (personal tax return including gig income)
- Report all quarterly estimated payments made during the year
- Pay any remaining balance or note any refund due

### April: plan for next year

- Review what deductions you claimed and whether any categories were underdocumented
- Adjust your quarterly estimated payment amounts for the coming year based on how this year's actual income compared to your estimates
- Evaluate whether your current mileage and expense tracking system worked well or needs improvement
- Consider whether a SEP-IRA or Solo 401(k) contribution makes sense for tax savings going forward

## Frequently asked questions

**How long does it take to set all of this up?**
The account openings and app downloads take two to three hours total spread across a few days. The ongoing weekly habits take five to ten minutes per week after initial setup.

**Do I need to do all of this even if gig work is just a weekend side hustle?**
The mileage tracking, tax savings habit, and insurance verification are non-negotiable even for part-time driving. The dedicated bank accounts are strongly recommended but technically optional if your volume is very low - though mixing personal and gig finances always creates extra work at tax time.

**What if I already started driving and have not done any of this?**
Start now rather than waiting. Retroactive mileage reconstruction is possible using your gig platform's trip history, though less accurate than real-time tracking. Open the accounts immediately and start the savings habit from this week's deposits forward. File your current year return with whatever records you have, then build better systems going forward.

**Is there a simple way to know if I am saving enough for taxes?**
If your dedicated tax savings account balance is growing each week and holds roughly 25-30% of your total gig earnings to date, you are on track. If the balance has not grown or you have borrowed from it for non-tax purposes, recalibrate immediately.

## Bottom line

The financial setup for gig driving takes a few hours to establish and a few minutes per week to maintain. The cost of not doing it - surprise tax bills, missed deductions, insurance gaps, and April stress - is far higher than the effort required upfront. Use this checklist as a literal step-by-step guide in your first week of driving and build the weekly habits that make the financial side of gig work manageable year-round.
