## Why expense tracking matters as much as mileage tracking

Mileage gets most of the attention in gig worker tax guides, but non-mileage expenses - hot bags, phone bills, car washes, supplies - add up to real deductions too. Without a system, receipts get lost in glove compartments and forgotten by tax season.

A dedicated expense tracking app solves this by letting you snap a photo of a receipt the moment you make a purchase, categorize it instantly, and generate a report months later without any reconstruction work.

## What to look for in an expense tracker

- Receipt photo capture with automatic data extraction
- Expense categorization matching Schedule C categories
- Bank or card account syncing to catch expenses you forget to log manually
- Export to CSV or PDF for tax filing or handing to a preparer
- Reasonable pricing for a gig worker's budget

## Top expense tracking apps compared

### 1. Stride

Already popular for mileage tracking, Stride also includes basic expense tracking for free.

**Strengths:**
- Completely free, no paid tier needed
- Simple photo receipt capture
- Combines mileage and expenses in one report
- Built specifically with gig workers in mind

**Limitations:**
- Less detailed categorization than dedicated expense apps
- No bank syncing

**Best for:** Drivers wanting one simple free app for both mileage and basic expenses.

### 2. QuickBooks Self-Employed

A more robust option built for freelancers and gig workers who want deeper financial tracking.

**Strengths:**
- Automatic bank and credit card syncing
- Separates business and personal transactions automatically using rules you set
- Generates quarterly tax estimates based on your actual income and expenses
- Direct export to TurboTax

**Limitations:**
- Costs around $15-20/month
- More features than casual drivers may need

**Best for:** Full-time gig drivers earning enough to justify the subscription cost through better organization and tax accuracy.

### 3. Expensify

Popular in the freelance and small business world, with a capable free tier.

**Strengths:**
- Excellent receipt scanning with automatic amount and merchant detection
- Free tier covers basic personal use cases
- Clean, fast interface

**Limitations:**
- Paid tiers get pricier if you need advanced features
- Not built specifically for gig driving categories

**Best for:** Drivers who also freelance in other ways and want one tool across multiple income streams.

### 4. Keeper Tax

Specifically designed around finding deductions gig workers commonly miss.

**Strengths:**
- Connects to your bank account and automatically flags potentially deductible transactions
- Asks simple yes/no questions to confirm business purpose
- Includes tax filing service as an add-on

**Limitations:**
- Subscription cost (around $20/month)
- Bank connection required, which some users are cautious about

**Best for:** Drivers who want an app to proactively find deductions rather than manually logging every expense themselves.

### 5. A simple spreadsheet (free, manual)

Do not underestimate this option. A basic Google Sheets template with columns for date, category, amount, and business purpose works perfectly well if you are disciplined about updating it weekly.

**Strengths:**
- Completely free
- Full control over categorization
- No app dependency or subscription

**Limitations:**
- Requires manual discipline
- No automatic receipt capture or bank syncing

**Best for:** Drivers with lower expense volume who prefer simplicity over automation.

## How to choose based on your driving volume

**Part-time (under 15 hours/week):** Stride or a simple spreadsheet is likely sufficient. Expense volume is low enough that manual tracking is manageable.

**Full-time (30+ hours/week):** Consider QuickBooks Self-Employed or Keeper Tax. The subscription cost is easily justified by time saved and deductions caught that you would otherwise miss.

**Multiple income streams (gig plus freelance work):** Expensify or QuickBooks Self-Employed handle diverse income and expense categories better than gig-specific apps.

## Setting up your system correctly regardless of app choice

1. Log every business expense the same day it happens, while the details are fresh
2. Take a photo of every receipt immediately, even if the app extracts the data automatically
3. Review and categorize weekly rather than letting expenses pile up
4. Export a report monthly to track your running total, not just at tax time
5. Cross-check your app's total against actual bank statements periodically to catch anything missed

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I switch expense apps partway through the year?**
Yes, though it helps to export your data from the old app first to keep a complete annual record in one place, or at least two clean partial-year reports.

**Do I need both a mileage app and a separate expense app?**
Not necessarily - apps like Stride combine both. If you use a mileage-only app like MileIQ, pairing it with a dedicated expense tracker or spreadsheet fills the gap.

**Is bank account syncing safe for these apps?**
Reputable apps use bank-level encryption (Plaid or similar services) for account connections. Read privacy policies if concerned, but this is standard practice across the fintech industry.

**What receipts should I keep even with an app tracking everything?**
Keep digital or physical copies of any receipt over $75, as some tax situations may require documentation beyond just the app's record in case of an audit.

## Bottom line

Stride remains the best free starting point for most gig drivers, combining mileage and basic expense tracking at no cost. Full-time drivers with higher expense volume should consider QuickBooks Self-Employed or Keeper Tax to justify the subscription through better organization and deduction discovery. Whatever you choose, the habit of logging expenses immediately matters more than which specific app you use.
