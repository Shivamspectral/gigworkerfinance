## DoorDash does not withhold taxes - but it does create deductions

When DoorDash pays you, no federal or state income tax comes out. No Social Security. No Medicare. You receive the full amount and handle taxes yourself at filing time.

The trade-off is that as a self-employed person you get to deduct ordinary and necessary business expenses on Schedule C. Those deductions reduce the income that self-employment tax and income tax apply to. For active Dashers, the total deductions often reach several thousand dollars per year.

This is the complete list for 2025.

## Mileage - your largest deduction

The IRS standard mileage rate for 2025 is 70 cents per mile. This covers gas, wear and tear, oil changes, tires, and depreciation in one simple number.

**What counts:**
- Miles driven from the moment you go online on the DoorDash app
- Miles to the restaurant to pick up the order
- Miles from the restaurant to the customer
- Miles between orders while you remain online and active
- Miles driven while waiting in a parking lot with the app open

**What does not count:**
- The drive from your home to where you start Dashing
- Personal errands between Dash sessions
- Miles after you end the Dash session

**How to track:** Use a free app like Stride that runs in the background automatically.

**Example:** 15,000 business miles x $0.70 = $10,500 deduction.

You cannot deduct both the standard mileage rate AND actual vehicle expenses. Pick one method and use it for the entire year.

## Phone and data plan

Your smartphone is a required tool for DoorDash. You cannot do the job without it.

**What you can deduct:**
- The business-use percentage of your monthly phone bill
- The business-use percentage of your data plan
- A phone mount or holder used for Dashing
- A portable charger used for Dashing

**How to calculate business use percentage:**
Track how many hours per month the phone is used for DoorDash versus total phone use. If you Dash 60 hours a month and use your phone 200 hours total, business use is 30 percent. Deduct 30 percent of your monthly bill.

Most active Dashers can justify 30 to 50 percent business use. Be honest and document your reasoning.

## Hot bags and insulated carriers

Hot bags, pizza bags, insulated carriers, and drink holders are required equipment for delivering food in good condition. They are 100 percent deductible as a business supply.

**What qualifies:**
- Insulated delivery bags
- Pizza delivery bags
- Drink carriers and cup holders
- Any bag purchased specifically for Dashing

Keep the receipt. These typically cost $20 to $60 each and are fully deductible in the year purchased.

## Car supplies and maintenance (if using actual expenses method)

If you chose the actual expenses method instead of standard mileage, you can deduct the business percentage of:

- Gas
- Oil changes
- Tire rotations and replacement
- Car washes (if used for deliveries)
- Brake repairs
- Any repair directly related to your Dash vehicle

Remember you must apply your business use percentage to these costs. If you use the car 40 percent for DoorDash, you deduct 40 percent of total vehicle costs.

Note: If you use the standard mileage rate, you cannot also deduct these expenses. The rate already includes them.

## Parking fees and tolls

Parking fees paid while making deliveries are deductible. Tolls paid while on a delivery are deductible. These are deducted separately from mileage, even if you use the standard mileage rate.

**What qualifies:**
- Parking meters paid during a delivery
- Parking garage fees during a delivery run
- Bridge and highway tolls during deliveries

Keep receipts or screenshot your payment confirmation.

## DoorDash equipment and accessories

Any gear you buy specifically to improve your Dashing operation is deductible:

- A better phone mount
- A backup battery pack
- Car charger for your phone
- Reflective safety vest (some drivers wear these)
- A second phone used only for Dashing

The item must be used for your Dash business. A general-purpose purchase that you occasionally use for Dashing does not fully qualify - apply the business-use percentage.

## Health insurance premiums

If you are self-employed and pay for your own health insurance, you may be able to deduct 100 percent of your premiums on Form 1040 (not Schedule C, but still reduces your taxable income).

This deduction applies if:
- You were not eligible for employer-sponsored insurance through a spouse or other job
- Your net self-employment income is positive for the year

This can be one of the largest deductions available to full-time gig workers.

## Retirement contributions

Contributing to a SEP-IRA or Solo 401(k) as a self-employed person reduces your taxable income dollar for dollar.

- SEP-IRA: Contribute up to 25 percent of net self-employment income, up to $69,000 for 2025
- Solo 401(k): Up to $23,500 employee contribution plus 25 percent employer contribution

These accounts let you build retirement savings while cutting your tax bill at the same time. A $3,000 SEP-IRA contribution in the 22 percent bracket saves $660 in income tax plus reduces the income subject to self-employment tax.

## Half of self-employment tax

Self-employment tax is 15.3 percent on net earnings. The IRS allows you to deduct half of that amount on Form 1040 as an adjustment to income.

This is calculated automatically by tax software. You do not need to figure it out manually. Just know it exists and that it partially offsets the sting of paying both sides of FICA.

## What you cannot deduct

- Speeding tickets or parking tickets (fines are never deductible)
- Traffic school costs
- Personal meals while Dashing (driving through a fast food restaurant for yourself is not a business meal)
- Clothing unless it has a logo and cannot be worn as regular clothing
- The DoorDash activation kit if DoorDash provided it for free

## How to keep records the IRS will accept

The IRS standard for records is that they be adequate to verify the deduction. For a Dasher that means:

- Mileage log with date, start, end, and business purpose for every trip
- Receipts for every equipment purchase
- Phone bill statements showing monthly amount
- Bank or credit card statements showing payment dates

A shoebox of receipts works. A spreadsheet works. An app that exports a PDF report works. What does not work is saying you think you drove about that many miles and guessing at expenses.

## Estimated deduction totals for an active Dasher

A Dasher doing this full time in a mid-size US city might see:

| Deduction | Estimated amount |
|---|---|
| Mileage (18,000 miles) | $12,600 |
| Phone (40% of $80/month) | $384 |
| Hot bags and supplies | $120 |
| Parking and tolls | $200 |
| Health insurance | $3,600 |
| Total | $16,904 |

That $16,904 in deductions on $45,000 of DoorDash income leaves $28,096 subject to self-employment tax and income tax. Without any deductions the bill would be on the full $45,000.

## Bottom line

Track your miles every single day. Save receipts for equipment. Note the business purpose of every purchase. These habits take five minutes per week and can reduce your tax bill by thousands of dollars. The deductions are real, they are legal, and the IRS expects self-employed people to claim them.
