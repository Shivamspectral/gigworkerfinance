## Filing as a DoorDash driver is different from a regular job

When you worked a W-2 job, filing taxes meant entering numbers from one form. As a DoorDash driver, you are running a small business, which means a few extra forms and calculations. This guide walks through the entire process step by step.

## Step 1: Gather your documents

Before you start, collect:

- Your 1099-NEC from DoorDash (available in the Dasher app or by mail, typically by January 31)
- Mileage log for the full year
- Receipts for equipment, phone bills, and supplies
- Records of any quarterly estimated tax payments you already made
- Last year's tax return, if available, for reference

## Step 2: Understand which forms you need

| Form | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Schedule C | Reports your DoorDash income and business expenses |
| Schedule SE | Calculates your self-employment tax |
| Form 1040 | Your main personal tax return |
| Form 1040-ES | Used for quarterly estimated payments (not filed with your return) |

## Step 3: Complete Schedule C

Schedule C is where you report your DoorDash business.

**Part I - Income:**
Enter your total gross income from your 1099-NEC (and any 1099-K if applicable) in the gross receipts line.

**Part II - Expenses:**
This is where deductions go. Common line items for Dashers include:

- Line 9 (Car and truck expenses): Your mileage deduction
- Line 22 (Supplies): Hot bags, phone mounts, delivery equipment
- Line 27a (Other expenses): Phone bill business-use percentage, parking, tolls

**Part IV - Vehicle information:**
You will answer questions about total miles driven, business miles, and whether you have written evidence to support your deduction (your mileage log).

The bottom of Schedule C shows your net profit or loss, which flows to both Schedule SE and Form 1040.

## Step 4: Complete Schedule SE

Schedule SE calculates self-employment tax based on your Schedule C net profit.

The calculation multiplies your net profit by 92.35%, then applies the 15.3% self-employment tax rate to that adjusted amount. Tax software does this automatically, but understanding the flow helps you spot errors.

You get to deduct half of this self-employment tax as an adjustment to income on Form 1040, which slightly reduces your overall taxable income.

## Step 5: Complete Form 1040

Your Schedule C net profit and half of your self-employment tax deduction both flow into Form 1040. From there, you calculate your regular income tax based on your total income, filing status, and any other deductions or credits you qualify for.

## Step 6: Report any quarterly payments already made

If you made estimated tax payments during the year, enter these on Form 1040 in the payments section. This reduces the amount you owe (or increases your refund) at filing time, since you already paid some of the tax throughout the year.

## Step 7: Choose how to file

**Option A: Tax software**
Programs like FreeTaxUSA, TaxSlayer Self-Employed, or TurboTax Self-Employed walk you through Schedule C and Schedule SE with guided questions. Most gig drivers find this the easiest and most affordable option.

**Option B: Hire a tax preparer**
Worth considering if you have multiple income sources, a complex situation, or simply want peace of mind. Costs typically range from $150 to $400 for a straightforward gig-worker return.

**Option C: File by hand**
Possible but not recommended unless you are very comfortable with tax forms. The risk of math errors or missed deductions is higher.

## Common filing mistakes to avoid

- Reporting only what appears on the 1099 and ignoring cash tips or platform bonuses not included on the form
- Forgetting to file a state return in states with income tax
- Claiming both standard mileage and actual vehicle expenses for the same vehicle in the same year (you must pick one method)
- Not including all 1099s if you drove for multiple platforms
- Missing the filing deadline (April 15 for most filers) without requesting an extension

## What if you owe more than you can pay?

File your return by the deadline regardless of whether you can pay in full. The failure-to-file penalty is significantly larger than the failure-to-pay penalty. After filing, set up an IRS payment plan online at irs.gov - most gig workers qualify for a short-term or long-term installment agreement with manageable monthly payments.

## Frequently asked questions

**When is the DoorDash tax deadline?**
The same as everyone else - April 15 for your annual return, plus quarterly estimated deadlines throughout the year if you owe $1,000 or more annually.

**Do I need to file if DoorDash was just a small side income?**
Yes, if your net self-employment income across all gig work is $400 or more, you are required to file and pay self-employment tax, even if it was not your main job.

**Can I get a refund as a DoorDash driver?**
Yes, if you overpaid through quarterly estimates or if your deductions significantly reduce your taxable income below what was already paid in.

**What if I made mistakes on a prior year return?**
File Form 1040-X to amend a previous return. This is common for gig workers who later realize they missed deductions like mileage.

## Bottom line

Filing DoorDash taxes involves Schedule C for your business income and expenses, Schedule SE for self-employment tax, and your regular Form 1040. Gather your documents early, choose reliable tax software or a preparer familiar with gig work, and double check that all your platforms' 1099s are included before submitting.
