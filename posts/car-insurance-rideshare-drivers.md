## Your personal auto policy probably does not cover you while driving for Uber or Lyft

This surprises a lot of new rideshare drivers. Standard personal auto insurance policies typically exclude commercial activity, and driving for Uber or Lyft counts as commercial use in the eyes of most insurers. If you get in an accident while the app is on and your insurer discovers this, your claim could be denied entirely.

## Understanding the three insurance periods

Rideshare driving splits into distinct periods, each with different coverage implications:

**Period 0: App off**
You are simply driving your personal vehicle. Your regular personal auto policy applies normally.

**Period 1: App on, waiting for a ride request**
You are logged in and available but have not accepted a trip. This is where the biggest coverage gap traditionally exists.

**Period 2: Trip accepted, en route to pickup**
You have accepted a ride and are driving to get the passenger.

**Period 3: Passenger in the vehicle**
The trip is actively underway.

Uber and Lyft provide contingent liability coverage during periods 1-3, but this coverage has limitations and does not include collision or comprehensive coverage for your own vehicle during Period 1 in many cases.

## What Uber and Lyft's insurance actually covers

**Period 1 (app on, no ride accepted):**
- Third-party liability coverage if you cause an accident: typically $50,000 per person injured, $100,000 per accident, $25,000 property damage
- This does NOT cover damage to your own vehicle

**Periods 2 and 3 (trip accepted through completion):**
- Up to $1 million in third-party liability coverage
- Contingent comprehensive and collision coverage (up to your vehicle's actual cash value, with a deductible, typically $2,500), but ONLY if you already carry comprehensive and collision on your personal policy

This last point matters enormously: if you dropped comprehensive and collision from your personal policy to save money, you may have no coverage for damage to your own car even during an active trip.

## Why you need a rideshare insurance endorsement

A rideshare endorsement (also called a rideshare rider) is an add-on to your personal auto policy that fills the Period 1 gap and ensures your own vehicle is protected throughout all periods, not just relying on the platform's contingent coverage.

**What it typically covers:**
- Fills the gap during Period 1 for both liability and physical damage to your car
- Often cheaper than a full commercial policy
- Keeps your existing personal insurer and policy structure

Costs vary based on your state, driving record, vehicle, and existing coverage levels. This is a modest addition compared to the risk of an uncovered accident.

## When you might need a full commercial policy instead

Rideshare endorsements typically only apply to rideshare driving (Uber, Lyft), not necessarily food delivery in all states, and some insurers cap the number of hours or trips covered under an endorsement. If you drive extensively across multiple platforms (rideshare plus DoorDash plus Instacart) for many hours weekly, a full commercial or "hybrid" rideshare/delivery policy may be more appropriate and can actually work out similarly priced once you account for all the coverage gaps an endorsement might not close.

## Delivery driver insurance considerations

DoorDash, Grubhub, and Instacart provide more limited insurance than rideshare platforms - often just liability coverage while you are on an active delivery, with no coverage during the period you are simply waiting for orders. Since delivery driving does not carry passengers, the risk profile is different, but the gap in Period 1 equivalent coverage is often even wider than with rideshare apps. A rideshare/delivery endorsement or commercial policy is worth strongly considering if delivery is a significant income source for you.

## What happens if you get in an accident without proper coverage

If you have an accident during Period 1 with only a standard personal policy (no endorsement) and your insurer discovers you were logged into a rideshare app:

- Your insurer may deny the claim entirely, citing commercial use exclusion
- You could be personally responsible for all damages and medical costs
- Your policy could be canceled or non-renewed once the insurer learns of undisclosed rideshare activity

This is a real financial risk, not a theoretical one - insurers actively investigate claims involving rideshare activity.

## How to add rideshare coverage correctly

1. Contact your current auto insurer and specifically ask about a "rideshare endorsement" or "TNC endorsement" (transportation network company)
2. Confirm whether the endorsement covers the platforms you actually drive for
3. Verify you still carry comprehensive and collision on your base policy, since the endorsement typically extends this coverage rather than replacing the need for it
4. Get the confirmation in writing or through your policy documents, not just a verbal assurance

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need special insurance for DoorDash if I already have rideshare coverage for Uber?**
Not automatically - confirm with your insurer whether your existing endorsement extends to delivery platforms or if you need a separate addition.

**Will adding a rideshare endorsement significantly increase my premium?**
Typically a modest increase (often $10-30/month) compared to the cost of a full commercial policy or the risk of an uncovered accident.

**Can I just rely on Uber and Lyft's insurance and skip a personal endorsement?**
Not recommended - the gap during Period 1, plus the requirement that you already carry your own comprehensive and collision for their contingent coverage to apply, means relying solely on the platform leaves real exposure.

**Does my insurance company need to know I drive for Uber even if I get an endorsement?**
Yes - full disclosure is important. Insurers who discover undisclosed rideshare activity can deny claims or cancel policies, even if you have what you believe is adequate coverage otherwise.

## Bottom line

Standard personal auto insurance has real gaps for rideshare and delivery drivers, particularly during the period when you are logged in but have not yet accepted a trip. A rideshare endorsement costing roughly $15-30 per month closes this gap for most part-time to moderate drivers, while heavy multi-platform drivers should evaluate a full commercial policy. Disclose your gig driving to your insurer regardless of which option you choose - an uncovered accident is a far larger financial risk than the modest cost of proper coverage.
