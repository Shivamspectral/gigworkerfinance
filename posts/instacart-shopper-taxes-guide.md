## Instacart shoppers face a unique tax situation

Instacart has two types of workers: in-store shoppers (who are often employees, W-2) and full-service shoppers (who are independent contractors, 1099). This guide focuses on full-service shoppers since that is where most tax confusion happens.

If you shop and deliver orders using your own car, you are almost certainly a 1099 independent contractor, meaning no taxes were withheld from your pay.

## Understanding your 1099 from Instacart

Instacart issues a 1099-NEC if you earned $600 or more during the year. This form only shows your gross earnings - it does not account for any expenses or deductions. You must calculate those yourself when filing.

If your total is below $600, you still owe tax on the income even without receiving a 1099. All self-employment income must be reported regardless of whether a form was issued.

## Mileage deduction for full-service shoppers

Full-service shopping involves driving to the store, shopping, then driving to the customer. All of these miles are deductible:

- Driving to the store to begin shopping
- Any miles driven between stores if splitting an order
- Driving from the store to the customer's address
- Miles driven while waiting for a new batch after a delivery, if you remain active in the app

**Not deductible:**
- Commute from home to your first shop of the day
- Personal errands before or after your shift

At 70 cents per mile for 2025, a shopper driving 12,000 business miles claims $8,400 in mileage deductions.

## In-store shopping time versus driving time

Since full-service shopping includes real time spent inside the store, some shoppers underestimate their mileage because they focus only on the driving portions. Remember that the drive TO the store for a specific order is deductible business mileage, not a commute, because you are actively working the moment you accept a batch.

## Supplies and equipment

- Insulated bags for cold or frozen items
- Reusable shopping bags used specifically for Instacart orders
- A phone mount for navigation during deliveries
- Furniture dolly or hand truck if you deliver bulk or heavy orders regularly

## Phone and data

Calculate the percentage of your phone use dedicated to the Instacart app, GPS navigation during shops, and customer communication. Most active shoppers can reasonably claim 30 to 45 percent of their monthly phone bill.

## Self-employment tax on Instacart income

Your net profit (income minus deductions) is subject to a 15.3 percent self-employment tax covering Social Security and Medicare, in addition to regular federal and state income tax.

**Example calculation:**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Gross Instacart income | $28,000 |
| Mileage deduction (10,000 mi) | $7,000 |
| Supplies and phone | $600 |
| Net profit | $20,400 |
| Self-employment tax (15.3%) | $2,876 |

## Quarterly estimated taxes

If you expect to owe $1,000 or more for the year, the IRS requires quarterly payments. Instacart shoppers commonly underestimate this because deposits arrive frequently and in smaller amounts, making it easy to spend the full amount without setting aside a tax reserve.

**Simple system:** Open a separate savings account and transfer 25 to 30 percent of every Instacart deposit immediately. Use this account exclusively for quarterly tax payments.

## Instacart Express and tips

Customer tips are part of your taxable income and are included in your 1099. There is no separate tax treatment for tips versus base pay in gig work - all of it counts as self-employment income.

## Common mistakes Instacart shoppers make

- Only tracking mileage for deliveries, forgetting the drive to the store counts too
- Not saving any money for taxes because deposits feel small and frequent
- Deducting 100% of a phone bill instead of the business-use percentage
- Missing quarterly tax deadlines because there is no employer reminder system
- Forgetting to report income below the $600 1099 threshold

## Frequently asked questions

**Am I an employee or contractor for Instacart?**
Full-service shoppers using their own vehicle are independent contractors (1099). In-store only shoppers are often W-2 employees. Check your specific role and pay stub structure to confirm.

**Can I deduct grocery bags I buy for myself to reuse on shops?**
Yes, if purchased specifically for Instacart use and not primarily for personal use.

**What if Instacart's 1099 amount does not match what I actually received?**
1099s sometimes include amounts before certain adjustments. Reconcile using your own weekly payment records and use your actual bank deposits as the source of truth if there is a discrepancy.

**Do I need a business license to shop for Instacart?**
Generally no for individual independent contractors, though some cities may have specific requirements - check local regulations if you are shopping full-time as a primary income source.

## Bottom line

Instacart shoppers are running a small delivery business every time they accept a batch. Track mileage from the store to the customer, save receipts for insulated bags and supplies, and set aside 25 to 30 percent of every payment for taxes. These habits turn a stack of small deposits into a legitimate, well-documented business with a manageable tax bill.
