## Why Stride is the recommended starting point for most gig drivers

Stride is completely free with no trip limits, making it the lowest-friction option for drivers just starting to track mileage and expenses properly. This guide walks through setup and daily use so you get the most out of it.

## Step 1: Download and create your account

Stride is available on both iOS and Android. Download it from your app store, create a free account using your email, and select that you drive for gig platforms when prompted during setup (this customizes some of the app's suggestions).

## Step 2: Enable location permissions correctly

This is the step most new users get wrong. When the app asks for location permission, select "Always Allow" rather than "While Using App."

**Why this matters:** If location access is only granted while the app is open, Stride cannot detect trips happening in the background while you use your gig apps (Uber, DoorDash, etc.) on top of it. "Always Allow" lets Stride track continuously without you needing to keep it open.

On iPhone: Settings > Privacy > Location Services > Stride > Always
On Android: Settings > Apps > Stride > Permissions > Location > Allow all the time

## Step 3: Turn on automatic mileage tracking

Inside the app, navigate to the tracking settings and enable automatic trip detection. Once enabled, Stride begins recording a trip whenever it detects you are driving, without requiring you to manually start and stop tracking.

## Step 4: Classify your trips daily

At the end of each driving session, open the app and review detected trips. Each trip needs to be classified as either:

- **Business:** Counts toward your tax deduction
- **Personal:** Excluded from your mileage log

Swipe or tap to classify each trip. Doing this daily takes about two minutes and prevents a backlog of unclassified trips that becomes tedious to sort through weeks later.

## Step 5: Log expenses as they happen

Within the app, use the expense tracking feature to snap photos of receipts immediately after a purchase - hot bags, phone accessories, car washes, or any other business-related cost. Categorize each one using Stride's built-in categories, which map reasonably well to Schedule C expense lines.

## Step 6: Review your dashboard regularly

Stride's dashboard shows your running mileage total and estimated deduction value in real time. Checking this weekly, rather than only at tax time, helps you understand your actual profit after deductions throughout the year - useful for adjusting your tax savings percentage if needed.

## Step 7: Export your annual report at tax time

When you are ready to file, navigate to the reports section and export your full-year mileage and expense summary, typically available as a PDF or CSV. This report includes:

- Total business miles for the year
- Estimated mileage deduction based on the current IRS rate
- Categorized expense totals
- Individual trip details if needed for backup documentation

Hand this report to your tax preparer or use it directly when filling out Schedule C in your tax software.

## Common setup mistakes to avoid

**Mistake 1: Granting only "while using" location permission**
This causes missed trips whenever your phone screen is off or Stride is running in the background, which is most of the time while actively driving.

**Mistake 2: Letting classified trips pile up**
Reviewing 200 unclassified trips after two months is overwhelming and increases the chance of misclassifying or forgetting the purpose of specific trips.

**Mistake 3: Not logging expenses in real time**
Waiting until tax season to reconstruct expenses from memory or scattered receipts defeats the purpose of having a tracking app at all.

**Mistake 4: Forgetting to export before switching phones**
If you get a new phone, make sure your Stride account data syncs to the cloud (it does automatically with an account, but confirm before wiping your old device) or export a backup report first.

## Tips for maximizing accuracy

- Start driving mode a few minutes before actually going online on your gig apps, since Stride's detection can sometimes take a moment to register motion
- If a trip is missing (rare, but possible with GPS gaps), manually add it using the app's manual trip entry feature, noting the date, distance, and purpose
- Periodically cross-check your Stride mileage total against your gig apps' own trip history for a sanity check, especially during your first few months of use

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Stride really completely free, with no hidden costs?**
Yes, Stride's core mileage and expense tracking features are free. The company generates revenue through optional insurance and financial product recommendations shown within the app, which you can ignore entirely.

**Does Stride work if I drive for multiple gig platforms?**
Yes, Stride is platform-agnostic - it tracks driving regardless of which specific app (Uber, DoorDash, Instacart, etc.) you are actively using during that trip.

**Can I use Stride alongside another tracking app?**
Technically yes, but running multiple GPS trackers simultaneously increases battery drain. Most drivers pick one primary tracking app to avoid duplicate data and phone performance issues.

**What if Stride misses a trip due to a phone issue?**
Use the manual entry feature to add the trip after the fact, including your best estimate of the date, mileage, and business purpose, based on your gig platform's trip history if needed for reference.

## Bottom line

Stride's biggest strength is that it costs nothing while covering both mileage and expense tracking adequately for most gig drivers. Set location permissions to "Always Allow," classify trips daily rather than letting them pile up, and export your annual report each tax season. This five-minute-a-day habit can protect thousands of dollars in legitimate deductions.
