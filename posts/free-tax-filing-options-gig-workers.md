## Yes, you can file self-employment taxes for free

A common misconception is that self-employment income automatically requires paid tax software or a professional preparer. Several genuinely free options exist for gig workers, particularly those under certain income thresholds, though the specific requirements vary by service.

## IRS Free File

The IRS partners with several tax software companies to offer completely free federal filing for taxpayers under a certain income threshold, adjusted annually (recently around $79,000 adjusted gross income, though check current year limits).

**How it works:**
- Visit irs.gov/freefile directly (not the individual software company websites, which may present paid options first)
- Choose from participating providers based on your specific situation
- Complete your return including Schedule C and Schedule SE for self-employment income
- Federal filing is free; state filing may or may not be included depending on the provider

**Limitations:**
- Interface quality varies significantly between participating providers
- Some providers have additional restrictions beyond the income threshold (age, state residency)
- Self-employment income support is included but interfaces are sometimes less polished than paid alternatives

## FreeTaxUSA

Not part of the official IRS Free File program, but offers genuinely free federal filing for all income levels, including self-employment.

**Strengths:**
- Free federal filing regardless of income level or complexity, including Schedule C
- State filing costs a flat fee (typically around $15), still cheaper than most alternatives
- Handles self-employment income, deductions, and quarterly payment tracking well
- Straightforward interface without aggressive upsells

**Limitations:**
- Less hand-holding and guidance compared to premium software like TurboTax
- Customer support is more limited than paid-tier competitors

**Best for:** Gig workers comfortable navigating tax forms with some independence, wanting genuinely free federal filing without income restrictions.

## Cash App Taxes

Completely free federal and state filing, including support for self-employment income and Schedule C.

**Strengths:**
- 100% free for both federal and state, no income restrictions
- Supports self-employment income, though the interface has historically been less robust for complex situations
- No upsells or paid tiers - it is genuinely free

**Limitations:**
- Fewer guided explanations for less experienced filers
- Support for more complex situations (multiple states, rental property, etc.) may be limited

**Best for:** Straightforward gig worker returns with one or two states and no unusually complex additional income sources.

## TaxSlayer Simply Free / Self-Employed tiers

TaxSlayer offers a genuinely free tier for simple returns, though most gig workers with Schedule C income will need to upgrade to their Self-Employed tier (a paid option, typically $50-65).

**Strengths of paid Self-Employed tier:**
- Strong guidance specifically for self-employment deductions
- Access to a tax professional for questions (varies by year and promotion)

**When free tier applies:**
Only if your return is simple enough to not require Schedule C, which is uncommon for active gig drivers, making this less relevant as a free option for most readers of this guide.

## Comparing your free options

| Service | Federal cost | State cost | Self-employment support | Income limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IRS Free File | Free | Varies by provider | Yes | ~$79,000 AGI |
| FreeTaxUSA | Free | ~$15 | Yes, well-supported | None |
| Cash App Taxes | Free | Free | Yes | None |
| TaxSlayer | Paid for Schedule C | Paid | Yes (paid tier) | N/A |

## When free filing might not be enough

Consider paying for more robust software or a professional preparer if:

- You have income from multiple states requiring complex allocation
- You formed an LLC or S-Corp and need more advanced business tax handling
- You have significant assets, investments, or rental property beyond gig income
- You simply want the peace of mind of professional review, especially in your first year of self-employment

## How to prepare before using any free filing service

Regardless of which option you choose, gather these first:

- All 1099s from every gig platform you worked for
- Your mileage log total for the year (from Stride, Everlance, or similar)
- Expense records categorized by type (supplies, phone, equipment)
- Any quarterly estimated tax payments already made, with amounts and dates
- Last year's tax return, if available, for reference and to check for consistency

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Cash App Taxes actually safe and legitimate for self-employment filing?**
Yes, it is a legitimate IRS-authorized e-file provider, despite the somewhat unusual branding tied to the Cash App payment platform.

**Can I switch tax software mid-filing if I start with one and it does not support my situation?**
Generally you would need to start over on a new platform since most services do not import partially completed returns from a different provider, though some allow importing prior year PDF returns for reference data.

**What if I qualify for IRS Free File but the specific provider's interface feels confusing?**
Try a different participating provider through the official IRS Free File portal, or switch to FreeTaxUSA or Cash App Taxes, which are open to all income levels regardless of the Free File income cap.

**Do free filing options handle quarterly estimated tax payment calculations for next year?**
Most, including FreeTaxUSA and Cash App Taxes, provide worksheets or estimates for next year's quarterly payments based on your current return, similar to paid alternatives.

## Bottom line

Gig workers do not need to pay for tax software by default. FreeTaxUSA and Cash App Taxes both offer genuinely free federal filing with solid self-employment income support regardless of income level, while IRS Free File works well if you fall under the income threshold and find a participating provider that fits your situation. Reserve paid software or professional preparers for genuinely complex situations beyond standard gig worker self-employment income.
