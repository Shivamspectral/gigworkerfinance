## When gig drivers need invoicing tools

Most rideshare and delivery driving does not require invoicing since payment happens automatically through the app. But many gig workers also take on side freelance work - vehicle wraps and local advertising deals, referral partnerships, or separate freelance gigs like handyman work, tutoring, or consulting alongside their driving income. This is where invoicing tools become necessary.

## What to look for in a free invoicing tool

- Professional-looking templates that build client trust
- Ability to track paid versus unpaid invoices
- Simple payment link integration (accepting cards or bank transfers)
- Export to PDF for record-keeping and tax purposes
- No cost or a genuinely usable free tier, not just a trial

## Top free invoicing tools

### 1. Wave

Wave offers completely free invoicing with no limits on the number of invoices sent, making it one of the most generous free tiers available.

**Strengths:**
- Unlimited free invoices
- Built-in accounting features beyond just invoicing (income and expense tracking)
- Accepts online payments (small transaction fee applies, similar to standard card processing rates)
- Clean, professional invoice templates

**Limitations:**
- Customer support is limited on the free tier
- Some advanced features (payroll, certain integrations) require paid add-ons

**Best for:** Gig workers who want invoicing plus basic bookkeeping in one free tool.

### 2. Invoice Simple

A straightforward, no-frills invoicing app focused purely on creating and sending invoices quickly.

**Strengths:**
- Extremely simple interface, invoice created in under a minute
- Free tier allows a limited number of invoices per month, sufficient for occasional freelance work
- Mobile app available for invoicing on the go

**Limitations:**
- Free tier has a monthly invoice cap (check current limits as they can change)
- Fewer bookkeeping features compared to Wave

**Best for:** Occasional freelance work alongside gig driving where you only send a few invoices monthly.

### 3. PayPal Invoicing

If you already use PayPal for payments, its built-in invoicing tool requires no separate signup.

**Strengths:**
- No separate account needed if you already use PayPal
- Clients can pay directly from the invoice via PayPal, card, or bank transfer
- Familiar and trusted by many clients

**Limitations:**
- Standard PayPal transaction fees apply when clients pay
- Less customizable templates compared to dedicated invoicing tools

**Best for:** Gig workers already using PayPal who want the simplest possible setup with no new account.

### 4. Square Invoices

Square offers free invoicing, particularly appealing if you also use Square for any in-person payment processing.

**Strengths:**
- Free to send invoices, standard processing fees only apply when payment is collected
- Integrates well if you already use Square hardware or point-of-sale for other work
- Automatic payment reminders for overdue invoices

**Limitations:**
- Best suited for those already in the Square ecosystem
- Less useful as a standalone tool if you have no other Square usage

**Best for:** Workers who also do in-person side work (like handyman services) and want unified invoicing and payment processing.

## What to include on every invoice

Regardless of which tool you use, professional invoices should include:

- Your name or business name and contact information
- Client's name and contact information
- Invoice number and date issued
- Clear description of services provided
- Amount due and payment terms (due upon receipt, net 15, net 30)
- Accepted payment methods

## Tracking invoices for tax purposes

Every invoice you send represents income that needs to be reported, whether or not the client issues you a 1099 (clients are only required to issue one if they paid you $600 or more in a year through certain payment methods). Keep copies of all invoices, paid and unpaid, as part of your income records alongside your gig platform 1099s.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to invoice for my rideshare or delivery driving income?**
No, gig platforms handle payment automatically. Invoicing tools are only necessary for separate freelance or side work outside the standard gig apps.

**Should invoice income be combined with gig driving income on my tax return?**
If the work is genuinely related (both are self-employment income), it often makes sense to report on the same Schedule C, or a separate one if the work is a distinctly different type of business - a tax preparer can advise based on your specific situation.

**What if a client does not pay an invoice on time?**
Most invoicing tools include automatic payment reminder features. Beyond that, having clear payment terms stated upfront (due dates, late fees if applicable) helps set expectations from the start.

**Are free invoicing tools professional enough for serious freelance clients?**
Yes, tools like Wave produce polished, professional-looking invoices indistinguishable from paid software in terms of client perception.

## Bottom line

Most gig drivers do not need invoicing tools for their primary rideshare or delivery income, but for any side freelance work, Wave stands out as the strongest free option combining unlimited invoicing with basic bookkeeping. Choose based on whether you need standalone simplicity (Invoice Simple, PayPal) or a more complete financial tracking solution (Wave, Square).
